Chapter One

The Year Everything Changed

Emily & James · Our Story

It started in college.

James and the girl who would later become such an important part of his life were studying together. At first, there was nothing unusual about it. They were just two students who saw each other regularly, attended classes, talked sometimes, and went on with their normal lives.

But slowly, things changed for James.

He started noticing her more than he noticed everyone else. He looked forward to seeing her at college. Small conversations started meaning more to him. Even when they were not talking, just having her around was enough to make his day feel different.

He realized that he had fallen in love with her.

So instead of keeping his feelings to himself, James decided to approach her. He told her how he felt, and after some time, she agreed to be with him.

For James, that was enough.

He did not enter the relationship expecting it to end. He genuinely believed he had found someone he could love and build something real with.

There was something James already knew, though.

Before him, she had been involved with one of his friends, and she had already hurt him by talking to another boy and crossing the boundaries of that relationship.

James knew about it.

He could have walked away.

He could have told himself that if she had done it once, she might do it again.

But James was in love, and he chose to believe that things would be different with him.

He accepted her anyway.

He gave her his time. He gave her attention. He tried to be there whenever she needed him. He spent money on her, helped her when he could, and kept forgiving things that hurt him because he did not want to lose the person he loved.

Whenever something happened between them, James tried to make things work instead of ending everything.

Maybe that was one of his biggest mistakes.

He kept believing that love meant giving someone another chance.

Then another.

And another.

But slowly, the same problems started appearing.

She talked to other boys, and James would get hurt. When he tried to talk about it, instead of feeling understood, he often ended up being blamed.

There were times when she made mistakes, but somehow James became the one who was wrong.

They would argue about it, and James would try to explain why he was hurt. He was not asking for perfection. He wanted honesty and respect from the person he had chosen to love.

But their arguments became more frequent.

The more James tried to hold on, the more tired he became.

Still, he stayed.

He had already invested so much of himself into the relationship that leaving did not feel easy. He remembered the good moments. He remembered why he had fallen in love with her in the first place.

So he kept hoping that things would get better.

They did not.

Eventually, the arguments became too much. One argument turned into another, and the same problems kept coming back. James felt like he was being blamed for things he had not done, while the things that hurt him were being ignored.

Then she called him rude and disrespectful.

And she left.

Just like that, the relationship that James had tried so hard to save was over.

For James, it was not simply losing a girlfriend.

It felt like losing all the time, money, effort, patience, and love he had given to someone who had never truly valued it in the same way.

He had accepted her even after knowing what had happened before him.

He had trusted her.

He had stayed when leaving would have been easier.

And in the end, he was the one left feeling like he had been used.

After the breakup, James changed.

He became quieter. He did not trust people the same way anymore. The things that had happened stayed in his mind, even when he tried not to think about them.

And then there was Emily.

Emily had been living in the same apartment building. James lived upstairs, while Emily lived downstairs. They had been around each other for some time, but James had never imagined that she would become such an important person in his life.

The funny thing was that Emily had liked James before.

James knew that she had some interest in him, but at that time, he did not feel the same way.

There were things about Emily's behavior that James did not like. He had his own reasons for keeping his distance, and he never thought of her as someone he would eventually fall in love with.

Life had other plans.

After his breakup, Emily was there.

She supported him.

She listened to him.

She did not try to force him to forget everything overnight. She simply stayed around and gave him the kind of attention he needed at a time when he was hurt.

Another girl who lived nearby also helped bring them closer.

Slowly, Emily and James started talking more.

At first, it did not feel like a love story.

It was just two people getting closer.

A conversation here.

A small joke there.

A little time spent together.

Then more.

James was still carrying the damage from his previous relationship, and without realizing it, he started bringing that pain into his relationship with Emily.

This is the part of the story James would understand much later.

The girl who had hurt him had changed the way he treated love.

He had complained about being ignored, but now he was the one who sometimes ignored Emily.

He had complained about not receiving time, but now he was the one making excuses.

He had wanted someone to stay when he was at his worst, but when Emily needed him, he sometimes left her alone.

There were times when Emily cried because of him.

And still, she stayed.

James did not understand her patience at first.

He thought she would eventually get tired and leave, just like everyone else.

But she did not.

She kept showing him that she cared.

She kept making an effort.

And slowly, something inside James started changing.

The first time Emily kissed him on the lips, it was more than just a kiss for him.

It was the moment he began to understand that his feelings for her were becoming real.

He had not expected it.

He had not planned it.

But he could not deny it anymore.

Emily was becoming important to him.

Their relationship was not perfect. They had their own problems, arguments, misunderstandings, and moments when things became difficult.

But there was something different about Emily.

She stayed.

They mostly met privately and kept many parts of their relationship between themselves. Even though their families knew about their relationship, there were still things that only Emily and James knew.

Over time, the two of them became closer.

James started realizing that Emily was not trying to replace what he had lost.

She was giving him something completely different.

Patience.

Time.

Care.

And a chance to become better than the person he had become after being hurt.

James had once believed that the biggest pain in his life was being left by someone he loved.

He did not know yet that another lesson was waiting for him.

Sometimes the person who saves you from the damage caused by someone else becomes the person you hurt because of that same damage.

Emily had entered his life when he was broken.

And without knowing it, she was about to become the person who taught him how to love again.

Chapter Two

What I Gave, What I Lost

Emily & James · Our Story

Before Emily became the person she is in my life today, there was a time when I believed that giving someone everything I had would make them stay.

I gave her my time. I gave her my money. I helped her whenever she needed me. I supported her in whatever way I could. I was always there for her, whether she needed someone to talk to, someone to take her somewhere, or simply someone who would be beside her.

I never counted what I was giving.

For me, it was love.

I thought that when you truly care about someone, you do not keep a record of what you spend or what you sacrifice. You just do it because that person matters to you.

We would go out to eat and drink three or four times a week. The total amount was always paid by me. At that time, I did not think much about the money. I was happy just being with her. I thought the time we spent together meant something to both of us.

But slowly, I started realizing that what meant so much to me did not have the same meaning for her.

There is one day I still remember clearly.

It was her birthday, but my mother was not well. Her condition became serious that day. I should have been with my mother. I should have stayed beside my family.

Instead, I went to her because it was her birthday.

I left my mother and went to her.

Later, my mother's condition became worse and she had to be hospitalized.

Even today, when I think about that day, I realize how far I had gone for someone who did not understand what I was sacrificing.

That was the kind of person I had become in that relationship. I was so attached that I was putting someone else before the people who had always been there for me.

I kept giving more and more, thinking that one day she would understand.

But she did not.

Then her friends started becoming a bigger part of our relationship.

They influenced her. They talked to her about me and slowly changed the way she looked at me. They had manipulated her so much against me that she stopped seeing the person I actually was.

The more distant she became, the harder I tried to hold on.

I begged her to stay.

I asked her not to leave.

There were times when I was ready to apologize even when I had not done anything wrong. I was willing to put my pride aside just to keep the relationship alive.

I would tell her I was sorry. I would ask her to forgive me. I would do anything I could to stop her from walking away.

There were moments when I felt like I was falling at her feet just to make her understand that I did not want to lose her.

Looking back, I realize that a relationship cannot survive on one person's effort forever.

For a while, it felt like the relationship was only alive because I was constantly trying to save it.

Then I stopped.

Not because I stopped caring.

I was simply tired.

And when I stopped making all those efforts, everything fell apart.

The relationship ended.

And when it ended, I did not just lose a girlfriend.

I lost the routine I had built around her.

I missed the conversations.

I missed the promises.

I missed going out together.

I missed eating together.

I missed walking together.

I missed the small things that had become normal parts of my life.

I remembered everything.

The places.

The conversations.

The time we had spent together.

The things we had promised each other.

At that point, I felt completely alone.

I cried a lot.

I was not myself anymore.

I had spent so much of myself on that relationship that when it ended, I did not know what was left of me.

I was hurting myself emotionally every day, thinking about what I had lost and wondering why everything I had done was still not enough.

I had given so much, but somehow I was the one left feeling worthless.

And then there was Emily.

Emily did not come into my life when everything was perfect.

She came when I was broken.

She saw me when I was at my lowest.

I was not the happy person I used to be. I was carrying anger, sadness, disappointment and memories from a relationship that had completely drained me.

And somehow, Emily stayed.

I cried in front of her.

I got frustrated.

I became difficult.

Sometimes I even took my frustration out on her without realizing how much it could affect her.

I was hurting, and because I was hurting, I became someone who could hurt other people too.

But Emily never made me feel like I was a burden.

She cared about me.

She listened to me.

She tried to understand me.

She helped me when I was struggling.

She would calm me down when I was upset.

She explained things to me when I could not understand them myself.

In a way, Emily started treating me like her own child.

I could be crying, angry, confused or completely lost, and she would still be there.

She never made me feel ashamed for being weak.

She never made me feel like I was too much.

She simply cared.

And that is something I still find difficult to explain.

Because there are some things a person does for you that cannot be measured with money.

You cannot put a price on someone sitting with you when you are broken.

You cannot put a price on someone listening to the same pain again and again.

You cannot put a price on someone staying when you are not easy to deal with.

Emily gave me something I did not even know I needed.

She gave me patience.

She gave me understanding.

She gave me care.

And most importantly, she gave me a reason to believe that not everyone would leave.

At that time, I was still comparing everything to my past.

I had been hurt so badly that I had started expecting people to hurt me again.

I had learned to protect myself by becoming distant.

I did not know how to receive love anymore.

I only knew how to fear losing it.

Emily had to deal with a version of me that was created by everything I had already gone through.

And instead of walking away, she tried to understand that version of me.

That is what makes her different in my story.

She did not save me with some big dramatic moment.

She saved me through small things.

Through her patience.

Through her words.

Through her presence.

Through simply being there.

I had spent so much time trying to prove my love to someone who never truly understood my value.

Emily never asked me to prove mine.

She just cared.

And slowly, without me even realizing it, she started becoming the person I could turn to when everything felt too heavy.

The strange thing is, at that time, I still did not fully understand what Emily was becoming in my life.

I only knew that whenever I was falling apart, she was there to help me stand again.

I thought I had lost everything after my first relationship ended.

I thought the person who left had taken something from me that I would never get back.

But I was wrong.

Because while I was looking at the person who had left, I failed to notice the person who had stayed.

Emily was there.

Quietly.

Patiently.

Without asking for anything in return.

And maybe that was the beginning of everything.

I had spent so much of my life believing that love meant giving everything you had to someone and hoping they would stay.

Emily slowly taught me something different.

Love could also mean staying when the other person had nothing left to give.

And I had no idea yet how important that lesson would become in our story.
