Chapter One

The Year Everything Changed

Emily & James · Our Story

It started in college.

James and the girl who would later become such an important part of his life were studying together. At first, there was nothing unusual about it. They were just two students who saw each other regularly, attended classes, talked sometimes, and went on with their normal lives.

But slowly, things changed for James.

He started noticing her more than he noticed everyone else. He looked forward to seeing her at college. Small conversations started meaning more to him. Even when they were not talking, just having her around was enough to make his day feel different.

He realized that he had fallen in love with her.

So instead of keeping his feelings to himself, James decided to approach her. He told her how he felt, and after some time, she agreed to be with him.

For James, that was enough.

He did not enter the relationship expecting it to end. He genuinely believed he had found someone he could love and build something real with.

There was something James already knew, though.

Before him, she had been involved with one of his friends, and she had already hurt him by talking to another boy and crossing the boundaries of that relationship.

James knew about it.

He could have walked away.

He could have told himself that if she had done it once, she might do it again.

But James was in love, and he chose to believe that things would be different with him.

He accepted her anyway.

He gave her his time. He gave her attention. He tried to be there whenever she needed him. He spent money on her, helped her when he could, and kept forgiving things that hurt him because he did not want to lose the person he loved.

Whenever something happened between them, James tried to make things work instead of ending everything.

Maybe that was one of his biggest mistakes.

He kept believing that love meant giving someone another chance.

Then another.

And another.

But slowly, the same problems started appearing.

She talked to other boys, and James would get hurt. When he tried to talk about it, instead of feeling understood, he often ended up being blamed.

There were times when she made mistakes, but somehow James became the one who was wrong.

They would argue about it, and James would try to explain why he was hurt. He was not asking for perfection. He wanted honesty and respect from the person he had chosen to love.

But their arguments became more frequent.

The more James tried to hold on, the more tired he became.

Still, he stayed.

He had already invested so much of himself into the relationship that leaving did not feel easy. He remembered the good moments. He remembered why he had fallen in love with her in the first place.

So he kept hoping that things would get better.

They did not.

Eventually, the arguments became too much. One argument turned into another, and the same problems kept coming back. James felt like he was being blamed for things he had not done, while the things that hurt him were being ignored.

Then she called him rude and disrespectful.

And she left.

Just like that, the relationship that James had tried so hard to save was over.

For James, it was not simply losing a girlfriend.

It felt like losing all the time, money, effort, patience, and love he had given to someone who had never truly valued it in the same way.

He had accepted her even after knowing what had happened before him.

He had trusted her.

He had stayed when leaving would have been easier.

And in the end, he was the one left feeling like he had been used.

After the breakup, James changed.

He became quieter. He did not trust people the same way anymore. The things that had happened stayed in his mind, even when he tried not to think about them.

And then there was Emily.

Emily had been living in the same apartment building. James lived upstairs, while Emily lived downstairs. They had been around each other for some time, but James had never imagined that she would become such an important person in his life.

The funny thing was that Emily had liked James before.

James knew that she had some interest in him, but at that time, he did not feel the same way.

There were things about Emily's behavior that James did not like. He had his own reasons for keeping his distance, and he never thought of her as someone he would eventually fall in love with.

Life had other plans.

After his breakup, Emily was there.

She supported him.

She listened to him.

She did not try to force him to forget everything overnight. She simply stayed around and gave him the kind of attention he needed at a time when he was hurt.

Another girl who lived nearby also helped bring them closer.

Slowly, Emily and James started talking more.

At first, it did not feel like a love story.

It was just two people getting closer.

A conversation here.

A small joke there.

A little time spent together.

Then more.

James was still carrying the damage from his previous relationship, and without realizing it, he started bringing that pain into his relationship with Emily.

This is the part of the story James would understand much later.

The girl who had hurt him had changed the way he treated love.

He had complained about being ignored, but now he was the one who sometimes ignored Emily.

He had complained about not receiving time, but now he was the one making excuses.

He had wanted someone to stay when he was at his worst, but when Emily needed him, he sometimes left her alone.

There were times when Emily cried because of him.

And still, she stayed.

James did not understand her patience at first.

He thought she would eventually get tired and leave, just like everyone else.

But she did not.

She kept showing him that she cared.

She kept making an effort.

And slowly, something inside James started changing.

The first time Emily kissed him on the lips, it was more than just a kiss for him.

It was the moment he began to understand that his feelings for her were becoming real.

He had not expected it.

He had not planned it.

But he could not deny it anymore.

Emily was becoming important to him.

Their relationship was not perfect. They had their own problems, arguments, misunderstandings, and moments when things became difficult.

But there was something different about Emily.

She stayed.

They mostly met privately and kept many parts of their relationship between themselves. Even though their families knew about their relationship, there were still things that only Emily and James knew.

Over time, the two of them became closer.

James started realizing that Emily was not trying to replace what he had lost.

She was giving him something completely different.

Patience.

Time.

Care.

And a chance to become better than the person he had become after being hurt.

James had once believed that the biggest pain in his life was being left by someone he loved.

He did not know yet that another lesson was waiting for him.

Sometimes the person who saves you from the damage caused by someone else becomes the person you hurt because of that same damage.

Emily had entered his life when he was broken.

And without knowing it, she was about to become the person who taught him how to love again.

Chapter Two

What I Gave, What I Lost

Emily & James · Our Story

There was a time when I thought love was something you had to earn.

I thought if I gave someone enough of my time, enough of my attention, enough of my money, enough of myself, they would eventually understand how much they meant to me.

I was wrong.

In 2025, I was in a relationship that slowly became everything I was willing to give and everything the other person was willing to take.

I gave her my time. I gave her my attention. I helped her whenever she needed me. I spent money on her without thinking twice. Three or four times a week, I would take her out to eat or have something to drink, and I would pay for everything. At that time, I never stopped to think about how much I was spending. I just wanted her to be happy.

I thought that was what love was supposed to look like.

If she needed something, I tried to help.

If she was upset, I tried to fix it.

If we had an argument, I was usually the one trying to make things right again.

Even when I knew I wasn't the one who had done something wrong, I would still apologize.

There were moments when I was so afraid of losing her that I was willing to put my pride aside completely. I would beg her not to leave. I would say sorry just to keep the relationship from falling apart. I would have done almost anything to make her stay.

Looking back now, I realize something painful.

A relationship cannot survive forever when only one person is carrying it.

Her friends had started influencing her. Slowly, the way she looked at me changed. The things I did for her no longer seemed to matter. The effort I was making became something she expected instead of something she appreciated.

And I kept trying harder.

I thought maybe if I gave more, things would go back to the way they were.

They didn't.

One memory still hurts when I think about it.

It was her birthday.

My mother was sick at the time, but I still left her and went to be with the girl I was in a relationship with. I thought her birthday was important. I thought being there for her was something I had to do.

Later, my mother was hospitalized.

Even now, when I remember that, I don't just remember what happened between me and that girl.

I remember what I was willing to sacrifice for someone who eventually left.

That is probably one of the hardest things about looking back.

It isn't only that someone left you.

It is realizing how much of yourself you gave before they did.

When the relationship finally ended, I didn't know what to do with all the feelings that were left behind.

I cried.

A lot.

I remembered the conversations we had, the places we went, the times we ate together, the walks, the promises, and all the little things that had once felt like they would last forever.

I kept thinking about everything I had done and wondering how something I had given so much to could end so easily.

For a while, I felt completely alone.

I had spent so much time trying to keep someone else from leaving that I had forgotten how to take care of myself.

And then there was Emily.

She came into my life at a time when I wasn't really okay.

She lived in the same apartment building. I was upstairs and she was downstairs.

The strange thing was that Emily had liked me before all of this.

But I didn't see her that way at first.

I had my own opinions about her. There were things about her behavior that I didn't like, and I never imagined that she would become the person who would mean so much to me later.

At that point, I wasn't looking for another relationship.

Honestly, I wasn't even sure I wanted to trust anyone again.

I was hurt.

I was angry.

And I had started believing that maybe caring too much about someone was just another way of giving them the power to hurt you.

Emily didn't try to force herself into my life.

She was just there.

When I was upset, she listened.

When I wanted to talk, she listened.

When I didn't know what to say, she still stayed.

I was carrying so much anger and sadness from what had happened that sometimes I didn't even know how to explain what was wrong with me.

But Emily didn't need me to explain everything perfectly.

She tried to understand me anyway.

There were times when I cried in front of her.

Times when I was frustrated.

Times when I was difficult.

And I know I wasn't always fair to her.

I had been hurt by someone else, but I was carrying that hurt into something new.

Sometimes I would ignore Emily.

Sometimes I would make excuses.

Sometimes I wouldn't give her the time she deserved.

There were moments when I probably made her feel like she wasn't important to me.

And she cried because of me.

That is something I can never pretend didn't happen.

The person who had come into my life when I was broken was now getting hurt because of the broken parts of me.

I didn't understand it fully at the time.

I was still too lost in everything that had happened before.

But Emily stayed.

She didn't suddenly become perfect, and our relationship wasn't perfect either.

We argued.

We misunderstood each other.

We had our own problems.

But there was something different about her.

She didn't make me feel like I had to earn her care.

She cared because she cared.

She would calm me down when I was upset.

She would explain things to me when I didn't understand.

She would listen even when I was repeating the same things again and again.

Sometimes it felt like she was taking care of me more than I deserved.

She treated me almost like I was her own child.

And even when I bothered her, even when I was difficult, she didn't make me feel worthless.

That meant more to me than I knew how to say.

There was a point when I started noticing the difference between what I had been chasing and what I actually needed.

I had spent so much time trying to convince someone to stay.

Emily never made me beg.

I had spent money trying to make someone happy.

Emily was happy just having me around.

I had given everything I could to someone who slowly stopped valuing it.

Emily was giving me something I couldn't buy.

Patience.

Understanding.

Care.

She was giving me a reason to believe that maybe love wasn't supposed to feel like constantly being afraid of losing someone.

Then came the moment that changed something inside me.

Our first kiss on the lips.

It wasn't some huge movie moment.

But for me, it meant something.

Because after everything I had been through, I realized that what I felt for Emily was real.

I wasn't talking to her just because I was lonely.

I wasn't staying because I needed someone to replace the person who had left.

I was beginning to love her.

And that scared me.

Because when you've already lost someone you gave everything to, loving someone again means giving them the same power to hurt you.

But this time, something was different.

Emily wasn't asking me to prove my love by destroying myself for her.

She was simply there.

And slowly, I started understanding what I had failed to understand before.

Love isn't always about how much you can give.

Sometimes love is about the person who stays beside you when you don't have anything left to give.

Emily had seen me at one of my lowest points.

She had seen the angry side of me.

The sad side.

The confused side.

The side that didn't know how to trust anymore.

And somehow, she still chose to stay.

I don't think I understood the weight of that choice back then.

I understand it now.

Because she didn't just become my girlfriend.

She became the person who helped me become myself again.

She taught me that being cared for doesn't always come with a price.

That someone can listen without expecting something in return.

That someone can stay without being begged.

And most importantly, she taught me that sometimes the person who comes into your life after you've been hurt doesn't deserve the punishment for what someone else did.

I wish I had understood that sooner.

Because Emily gave me the kind of patience I had once begged someone else for.

And while she was trying to heal the parts of me that someone else had broken, I was sometimes hurting her with those same broken parts.

That is something I will always regret.

But maybe that is what made our story real.

We weren't perfect.

We didn't suddenly become the perfect couple.

We were two people trying to understand each other while carrying our own problems, our own mistakes, and our own fears.

And somewhere in all of that, I realized something.

I had spent so much time thinking about the person I lost that I almost failed to see the person who had stayed.

Emily was there when I had nothing left.

And somehow, she made me feel like maybe I hadn't lost everything after all.

Maybe I had just lost the wrong person.

And found the right one.
